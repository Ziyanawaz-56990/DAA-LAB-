# References — Collatz Conjecture

All entries below were checked against a publisher, university, journal, arXiv or other repository page while preparing this folder (searches in October 2026). Where a detail (volume, pages, DOI) was **not** visible on a page we checked, it is **left out** rather than guessed. Please re-check any citation yourself before formal submission.

**Title:** The 3x + 1 Problem and Its Generalizations
**Author(s):** Jeffrey C. Lagarias
**Year:** 1985
**Publication / Repository:** The American Mathematical Monthly, vol. 92, no. 1, pp. 3–23 (Lester R. Ford Award 1986). DOI 10.2307/2322189
**Link:** https://maa.org/node/113776  (DOI: https://doi.org/10.2307/2322189)
**Why it is relevant:** the classic survey of the history, names, partial results and generalisations of the problem.
**What it helps us understand:** the basic definitions, why the problem has so many names, and what was known by 1985.

**Year:** 2010

**Title:** A stopping time problem on the positive integers
**Author(s):** Riho Terras
**Year:** 1976
**Publication / Repository:** Acta Arithmetica 30 (1976), pp. 241–252. DOI 10.4064/aa-30-3-241-252
**Link:** https://www.impan.pl/en/publishing-house/journals-and-series/acta-arithmetica/all/30/3/101028/a-stopping-time-problem-on-the-positive-integers
**Why it is relevant:** introduced the *stopping time* viewpoint used in our program.
**What it helps us understand:** why "almost all" numbers have finite stopping time and why that is not enough for a full proof.

**Title:** Iteration of the number-theoretic function f(2n) = n, f(2n+1) = 3n+2
**Author(s):** C. J. Everett
**Year:** 1977
**Publication / Repository:** Advances in Mathematics (journal details as cited in the bibliographies of Tao's paper and the MathWorld Collatz Problem page; page numbers not re-checked)
**Link:** https://mathworld.wolfram.com/CollatzProblem.html  (where the reference is listed)
**Why it is relevant:** independently of Terras, proved that almost all integers have a trajectory that drops below the start value.
**What it helps us understand:** the early "almost all" results.

---

**Title:** Bounds for the 3x+1 Problem using Difference Inequalities
**Author(s):** Ilia Krasikov, Jeffrey C. Lagarias
**Year:** 2003
**Publication / Repository:** Acta Arithmetica 109 (2003), pp. 237–258. DOI 10.4064/aa109-3-4. arXiv:math/0205002
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0205002
**Why it is relevant:** proves that at least x^0.84 of the integers up to x have 1 in their orbit.
**What it helps us understand:** how mathematicians prove *lower bounds* on how many integers converge (a long way from "all").

---

**Title:** Almost all orbits of the Collatz map attain almost bounded values
**Author(s):** Terence Tao
**Year:** 2022 (arXiv first posted 2019)
**Publication / Repository:** Forum of Mathematics, Pi (2022); arXiv:1909.03562
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03562
**Why it is relevant:** the strongest known "almost all" result for the Collatz map (logarithmic density).
**What it helps us understand:** exactly what is known about typical start values and what is not (it does not cover every integer).

**Title:** There are no Collatz m-Cycles with m ≤ 91
**Author(s):** Christian Hercher
**Year:** 2023
**Publication / Repository:** Journal of Integer Sequences, vol. 26 (2023), Article 23.3.5 (a corrigendum to the article is also published on the journal site). arXiv:2201.00406
**Link:** https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/journals/JIS/VOL26/Hercher/hercher5.html
**Why it is relevant:** a result about possible non-trivial cycles; it also shows how verification limits feed into theory.
**What it helps us understand:** why a non-trivial cycle, if it exists, must be very large.

**Title:** Convergence verification of the Collatz problem
**Author(s):** David Bařina (Barina)
**Year:** 2021
**Publication / Repository:** The Journal of Supercomputing (2021). Record page: https://www.vut.cz/en/rad/results/detail/168171
**Link:** https://www.fit.vut.cz/research/publication-file/12315/postprint.pdf  (author's postprint)
**Why it is relevant:** an efficient algorithm (small lookup tables, CPU and GPU) for verifying convergence of huge ranges of start values; also tracks "path records".
**What it helps us understand:** how real verification differs from our simple lab loop (128-bit arithmetic, sieving, parallel hardware).

---

**Title:** Improved verification limit for the convergence of the Collatz conjecture
**Author(s):** David Bařina (Barina)
**Year:** 2025
**Publication / Repository:** The Journal of Supercomputing, vol. 81 (2025), article 810. DOI 10.1007/s11227-025-07337-0
**Link:** https://www.fit.vut.cz/research/publication/c197809  (project page: https://pcbarina.fit.vut.cz/)
**Why it is relevant:** reports verification of all start values up to 2^71 and four new path records.
**What it helps us understand:** the current computational frontier and that it is still *finite* evidence.

**Title:** The Undecidability of the Generalized Collatz Problem
**Author(s):** Stuart A. Kurtz, Janos Simon
**Year:** 2007
**Publication / Repository:** Theory and Applications of Models of Computation (TAMC 2007), Lecture Notes in Computer Science vol. 4484, Springer, pp. 542–553. DOI 10.1007/978-3-540-72504-6_49
**Link:** https://link.altmetric.com/details/904862  (bibliographic record); also listed at https://mathworld.wolfram.com/CollatzProblem.html
**Why it is relevant:** proves that a natural *generalisation* of the Collatz problem is undecidable.
**What it helps us understand:** the "computability" remark in the lab question. (MathWorld notes that this proof does **not** apply to the original 3n+1 problem.)
